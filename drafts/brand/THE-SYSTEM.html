One direction, not a menu. Every value below is measured and passes AA in both modes.

The constraint that decides everything

Capso's canvas is full of other people's screenshots. A Stripe pricing page, a purple dashboard, a green empty state — colour you do not control, arriving all day. That single fact settles most of the design questions, and it is why the earlier Riso direction was wrong for the product even though it was the most beautiful board.

The interface must be nearly colourless so the captures carry the colour. White walls, not a patterned gallery. Brand colour lives only where content cannot: the mark, onboarding, empty states, marketing.

It also settles the accent argument in an unexpected way. Blue, purple, green and black are the most common accent colours inside SaaS screenshots. Orange is rare. So a warm terracotta is not the safe default choice here — it is the functionally correct one, because it stays distinguishable from the content it sits beside all day.

The rule that makes it a system

Warm means Capso is speaking. Cool means your content is being described. The accent is reserved — buttons, focus, the product's own voice. It never labels your material.

Intent palette — your content's taxonomy

Capso already classifies every capture into seven intents, and today renders all seven as identical grey chips. That taxonomy is the obvious home for the secondary palette: it is information the product already has, currently thrown away visually.

The mark

A capsule you can see into

The upper half is the shell — constant, terracotta, the product. The lower half is a window holding three captured cards, and those cards are drawn in the intent colours. So the mark is literally the product's data model.

It is the only form where mascot, logo and app icon are one object, and it survives a rename, because it is a capsule rather than the letters C-A-P-S-O.

On a tile, which is how icons actually ship

The three cards collapse to one light block below about 32px. That degradation is designed, not accidental — at favicon size the mark is a capsule with a face and nothing else, which is the correct amount of information for 16 pixels.

Typography

Interface

Keep the system stack.

No webfont, no layout shift, native rendering, zero bytes. Scale is 12/14/16/20/28 — now implemented. The UI's job is to disappear.

Brand

One warm display serif — wordmark, marketing headlines, onboarding. Never inside the app.

Fraunces (variable, free) as first choice; Instrument Serif as the lighter alternative.

A serif is the real differentiator here. Every competitor in this category is sans-everything. A memory product has genuine licence to feel like an archive or an editorial object rather than a utility, and a serif wordmark against a system-sans UI says that in one move — while costing nothing in the interface itself.
