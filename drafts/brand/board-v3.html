Fresh colour & a 2026 direction

Five colour worlds carrying the same capsule mechanism, built from the three trend sources. Terracotta is off the table — but the real finding is that swapping one accent for another is itself the dated move.

1 Aug 2026Recraft V4.1 standard5 worlds · 10 rendersNo files under apps/ touched

The finding that matters

Fixed single-accent palettes are the thing that dates a brand. The move is a palette system.

Both branding sources say the same thing independently: The Branding Journal calls for "variable colour themes that can shift and evolve", recognisable by colour mood rather than a strict palette; Outcrowd calls rigid guidelines and one-dimensional systems explicitly dated, in favour of modular constructor frameworks. So the answer to "fresh colour" is not a different hex in the --accent slot. It is a constant, quiet shell plus contents that shift — which is precisely what the loaded-capsule mechanism already wanted.

What actually applies to Capso

Thirteen-plus trends across the three sources. Most are noise for a screenshot tool. These five are load-bearing.

Flexible palettesShell constant, contents variable. Solves the colour question and the project-thread question with one decision.
Imperfect print / analog-digital hybridGrain, misregistration, scanned texture. Thematically exact: a screenshot is a scan of a screen. Also the sharpest break from flat-vector SaaS default.
Living brand charactersMarks with calm / active / focused states. capture.tsx:268-275 already has four states — loading, suggestion, confirmed, timeout. The hook exists.
Modular identity systemsCombinable elements over fixed templates. One capsule per project thread.
Anti-AI / human touchMicro-imperfection and asymmetry as a trust signal. Directly counters the flat-vector look round one produced.

Deliberately ignored: neo-brutalism and maximalism (wrong for a calm memory tool), gamification, 3D/WebGL, retrofuturism, neumorphism (dated on arrival), and every AI-agent trend — those are product strategy, not identity.

Five worlds

Riso

recommended

Three fluorescent inks on newsprint, with visible grain and misregistration.

  • Hits three trends at once: imperfect print, analog-digital, human touch
  • Warm and genuinely funny without being childish
  • Overprint gives a fourth colour free where inks overlap
  • Risk: fluoro inks are for the brand layer, not the working UI

Dopamine

best system demo

Saturated shells — violet, lime, cobalt — each with different cargo.

  • Shows the flexible-palette idea most literally: shell varies, mechanism holds
  • Joyful and immediately legible
  • Risk: fully saturated shells fight a calm working surface, and the big cartoon eyes read generic

Cold storage

premium, cold

Graphite shell, smoked glass, contents glowing cyan and magenta on near-black.

  • The most distinctive and most premium of the five
  • "Archived data" reads instantly
  • Risk: dark-native, so it fights the app's light default — and it is not fun, which was the brief

Glass / specimen

beautiful, not a mark

Frosted translucent capsule, cards suspended and refracted inside.

  • Nails the sensory/tactile trend — glassy, waxy, almost touchable
  • Risk: it is a 3D render, not a mark. No character, and nothing survives reduction to an icon
  • Keep it for hero imagery, not identity

Fluid adaptive

rejected

Soft-edged melting mark, mid-transformation.

  • The capsule dissolves into an amoeba — the form is gone
  • Leans on gradient mesh, which the Design Brain lists as an anti-slop cliché: "random gradients as an automatic synonym for modern"
  • The one trend I would actively avoid

Recommendation

Riso palette, flexible shell — brand layer loud, working UI quiet.

Riso wins the brand layer. But nobody can work all day inside fluorescent pink, so the system splits: the inks carry the mascot, onboarding, marketing and the OG card. The app itself keeps one ink as its accent and lets captured content supply the rest of the colour. That is the flexible-palette trend applied honestly rather than decoratively.

Blue, pink and yellow are true Riso ink values. Overprint is what pink and blue actually make where they overlap — a real fourth colour you get for free, not an invented one. Newsprint replaces #fafaf8.

Contrast audit — one finding changes the architecture

No single blue works in both modes. Federal Blue is superb on newsprint and nearly invisible on dark. Measured, not estimated:

PairRatioVerdict
Federal Blue on newsprint8.88AAA — light-mode accent
Federal Blue on #1414141.73fails badly — unusable
Lifted blue on #1414145.22AA — dark-mode accent
White on Federal Blue10.64AAA — button labels safe
Overprint violet on newsprint5.51AA — safe for text
Pink on newsprint2.57fill only, never text
Yellow on newsprint1.04decorative only

So the accent has to be mode-dependent — #1f28ae light, #6b7cff dark. That is a real change from today's architecture, where globals.css:12-20 deliberately leaves --accent un-overridden in dark because terracotta happens to survive both. Losing that one-line simplicity is the actual cost of leaving terracotta, and it is worth naming before you decide.
