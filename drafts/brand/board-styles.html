Five style registers

Same capsule, same locked palette, same prompt structure in all five. Style is the only variable, so what you are comparing is genuinely style and not colour or subject.

What the evidence says before you look

A Mobbin sweep for mascots inside working product interfaces returned a consistent pattern, and it constrains this choice more than taste does.

Almost nobody puts a mascot in the working surface. Alan's 3D bear appears on the login screen and inside a modal — never the dashboard. Gusto's pig is a loading state. Homerun's dog is a thank-you screen. Ferndesk's character sits in a setup panel. Duolingo is the single exception, and Duolingo is a game.

Capso's UI is deliberately restrained and its canvas is full of other people's screenshots. So the register does not need to survive the dashboard — it needs to be excellent at the edges, and reducible to an icon. Those are different jobs, and they may want different assets.

The five

1 · Soft 3D / clay

marketing winner

Matte clay shell, dark eyes, translucent lower half with the cards genuinely visible through it. Photographs like a product.

  • Best hero, OG card and App Store asset of the five by some distance
  • The translucency sells the whole concept in one image
  • Dimpled surface reads slightly organic — closer to a seed pod than a manufactured capsule
  • Cannot become the icon; needs a flat twin kept in sync
  • The most crowded register in AI-era software

Ships as: Alan, Digg, Google Meet avatars. All at the edges, none in the working UI.

2 · Flat vector

recommended core

Solid shell, clean window, three cards in the intent colours. The right-hand variant is the stronger one — no hairline, so nothing to lose at small sizes.

  • It is the icon. One SVG feeds favicon, extension, tray and mascot with nothing to sync
  • Native to the UI already shipping — same flat, hairline, unshaded language
  • Only register that survives 16px without a redraw
  • Came back closest to the locked accent: #c1461e against #c2461f
  • Least to look at in a hero — which is exactly what register 1 is for

Ships as: Brilliant's Koji, Sora's blob. Both appear once at onboarding, then never again.

3 · Riso / hand-drawn

warmest

Halftone grain, wobbly confident line, ink that overprints. The left one works; the right one gave the character a bandit mask.

  • Most human of the five, and the clearest trend signal from the 2026 sources
  • Excellent on stickers, merch and onboarding
  • Grain fights the precise hairline UI — brand layer only, never in-app
  • Drifted warm on the shell: #c1632e rather than #c2461f

Ships as: Typeform's dog, Charma's blob — single empty states, nowhere else.

4 · Toy / collectible

best for the project family
Second variant
failed to generate

Heavy keyline, die-cut edge, body packed with cards. Built to be duplicated — one capsule per project.

  • Strongest fit for the per-project capsule family, the best structural idea in the whole exploration
  • Obvious sticker sheet and merch
  • Skews youngest; the oversized eyes read closer to a game character than a work tool
  • Shell drifted furthest from the locked accent

Ships as: Duolingo. The one product that genuinely puts its mascot in the working surface — because it is a game.

5 · Editorial / engraved

most original, least usable

Cross-hatched cap over a glass chamber, presented as a catalogue specimen. The framed variant is a genuine idea: your captures as archived specimens.

  • Uncontested. No competitor in this category is anywhere near this
  • The only register that says "archive", which is what a memory product actually is
  • Pairs naturally with the serif logotype
  • Cold — hard to make it feel friendly, and you asked for fun
  • Hatching disappears entirely below ~64px; it can never be the icon
  • A third asset with no home in the product

Ships as: nobody, in this category. That is the appeal and the risk.

Recommendation

Flat vector as the core. Clay as its marketing photograph. Two assets, not five.

Why not one register for everything: the Mobbin evidence shows the mascot's real job is at the edges — login, loading, empty, onboarding, the store listing. That job wants clay. The icon job wants vector. Forcing one register to do both makes it mediocre at each.

Why vector is the core rather than clay: it is the only one that is already the icon, already matches the shipped UI, already colour-accurate, and already survives 16px. Everything downstream — build_icons.py, the favicon, the extension, the tray template — regenerates from that one SVG with nothing to keep in sync.

What I would drop: riso and toy are both good and both are a third asset with no home. Riso survives as a texture treatment on merch if you ever want it; it should not become the mascot.

The one I would keep arguing for: editorial. It is the only genuinely uncontested territory here, and "your captures as catalogued specimens" is a better story than anything else on this page. It loses because it cannot reduce and it is not warm — but if you want the brand to be interesting over safe, say so and I will build that instead.
